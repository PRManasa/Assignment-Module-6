## Part 3: Strategic Prompting Techniques
### 3.1 Chain-of-Thought Prompting

#### Problem Selected

A company has 150 employees. 60% work in engineering, 25% in sales, and the rest in operations. If the company plans to hire 20% more engineers and 10% more sales people, how many total employees will there be after hiring?

#### Without Chain-of-Thought
Prompt: A company has 150 employees. 60% work in engineering, 25% in sales, and the rest in operations. If the company plans to hire 20% more engineers and 10% more sales people, how many total employees will there be after hiring? Give only the final answer.
Response: 171.75 employees

#### With Chain-of-Thought
Prompt: A company has 150 employees. 60% work in engineering, 25% in sales, and the rest in operations. If the company plans to hire 20% more engineers and 10% more sales people, how many total employees will there be after hiring? Solve this step by step. 
Response: Let's solve it using the numbers given.

### Step 1: Calculate current employees in each department

* Engineering = 60% of 150
  = 0.60 × 150
  = **90**

* Sales = 25% of 150
  = 0.25 × 150
  = **37.5**

* Operations = Remaining employees
  = 150 − 90 − 37.5
  = **22.5**

### Step 2: Calculate new engineering hires

The company hires 20% more engineers:

* New engineering hires
  = 20% of 90
  = 0.20 × 90
  = **18**

### Step 3: Calculate new sales hires

The company hires 10% more sales employees:

* New sales hires
  = 10% of 37.5
  = 0.10 × 37.5
  = **3.75**

### Step 4: Calculate total employees after hiring

* Current employees = 150
* Total new hires = 18 + 3.75 = **21.75**

Therefore:

* Total employees after hiring
  = 150 + 21.75
  = **171.75**

**Answer: 171.75 employees**

*Note: The result is not a whole number because the original percentages imply 37.5 sales employees and 22.5 operations employees, which is not realistic for actual employee counts.*

#### Comparison

The first prompt gave only the final answer, so it was quick but not easy to verify. The step-by-step response showed how the number was calculated, which made it easier to catch the issue that employee counts became decimals. Chain-of-thought helped because the problem had more than one calculation step. One limitation was that the response became longer, and the final answer still needed common sense because employees are normally counted as whole people.

### 4.3 Limitations & Responsible Use
One limitation noticed was that LLMs may sometimes give extra information that is not directly needed. In the hallucination test, the model did not fully make up the award, but it still gave unrelated references around the topic.

Another limitation was that the output changed based on small changes in the prompt. When more context or instructions were added, the response became more controlled, but it also became more dependent on how the prompt was written.

A third limitation was that LLMs may still reflect bias if the prompt is too broad or if the training data contains stereotypes. Even when no clear bias appeared in the test, the output still needed to be checked carefully.

LLM outputs should be verified when the information is factual, sensitive, or used for important decisions. They should not be used as the only source for medical, legal, financial, or safety-related decisions.

LLMs should be used ethically by being transparent about AI assistance, checking important information, and avoiding harmful or misleading use.
